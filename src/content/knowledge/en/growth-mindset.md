## The core distinction

Carol Dweck's 30 years of research keeps returning to one contrast:

- **Fixed mindset**: "Smartness is given. I need to prove I'm smart."
- **Growth mindset**: "Ability is built. I can get better with practice."

## A single sentence can shift everything

| Fixed | Growth |
|---|---|
| "You're so smart" | "You stuck with it — that was real effort" |
| "I failed the test — I guess I'm not cut out for this" | "That approach didn't fit me — I'll try another" |
| "I don't want anyone to see I can't do this" | "What I can't do yet is exactly what I will learn" |

## Why it matters

People in a fixed mindset, after failing, tend to attribute it to "I'm not good enough" and avoid challenges.
People in a growth mindset attribute it to "this method didn't work" and try another.

## A practice

Find one thing you almost gave up on. Ask yourself:

> "If I explained this failure as 'I encountered a problem I can't solve *yet*' instead of 'I'm not built for this' — what would I do next?"

Write down the answer. That sentence is your growth-mind brain waking up.
