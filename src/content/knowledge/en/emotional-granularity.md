## A counter-intuitive finding

You might assume "what I feel" is given. But emotion scientist Lisa Feldman Barrett's research suggests:

> **Emotion is not a passive "signal" — it is a concept constructed by your brain.**

What determines how many versions of emotion you can construct is, in part, your **emotion vocabulary**.

## A side-by-side

| Person A | Person B |
|---|---|
| "I feel bad today" | "I felt let down by my colleague not replying to my email, and a bit of anger at being overlooked" |

Person B recovers faster — not because they're smarter, but because they saw "what's actually going on" earlier.

## How to practice

- Build a 30-word "emotion dictionary"
- Each day, name 3 moments (even "I feel slightly *resentful*, but not quite *dejected*")
- Don't aim for the "right" word — naming is the practice

## A small caveat

This is a skill, not a gift. It requires practice like a language. **The payoff, however, is large**: your ability to regulate mood improves noticeably.
