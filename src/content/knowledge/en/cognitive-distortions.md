## What is a cognitive distortion

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) discovered: when our emotions are loud, what shows up as "facts" inside our head is often **a conclusion**, not **the evidence for it**.

The idea of cognitive distortions comes from Aaron Beck's cognitive therapy; David Burns popularised a list of 10 common types in *Feeling Good* (1980), and later authors extended it. A quick reference (names and groupings vary between books):

| Distortion | Family | Plain meaning |
|---|---|---|
| All-or-nothing | Black/white | "Either perfect or a failure" |
| Overgeneralization | One -> all | "This one miss = I always fail" |
| Mental filter | Selective attention | Notice only the negative |
| Disqualifying the positive | Discount wins | "That success was just luck" |
| Jumping to conclusions | Mind-reading / fortune-telling | "No reply = they hate me" |
| Magnification / minimization | Distorted scale | Inflating small mistakes, deflating big wins |
| Emotional reasoning | Feeling-as-fact | "I feel hopeless = I am hopeless" |
| Should statements | Harsh rules | "I should / must / shouldn't" |
| Labeling | Global identity | "I'm a fraud" |
| Personalization | Self-blame | "The team failed because of me" |
| Blame (reverse) | Other-blame | "When I do well it's luck, when others do well it's skill" |
| Fallacy of fairness | Comparison | "Effort should be rewarded instantly" |

## The first step: name it

Next time a strong feeling shows up, quietly ask: **"Which one just showed up?"**

Naming a thought often gives you a little distance from it — it is a thought, not necessarily a fact.

## A practice

Pick one distortion you used recently. Write down:
- The triggering event
- The "fact statement" in your head
- How you would name it now
- A more balanced alternative

Keep at it and you will spot them faster in the moment. If negative thoughts dominate your life or affect sleep or work, please seek professional help.
